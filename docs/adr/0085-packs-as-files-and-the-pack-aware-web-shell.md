# ADR 0085: the pack-aware web shell

Status: **ACCEPTED** by the owner's direction of 2026-10-14 (the Stack19 blueprint, sections 2 to 5 and 13; job CL, first built against the contract sketch, then switched to job CK's real pieces). **This ADR is only about the web.** What a business type is, the schema, the closed lists, the modules and their database guards, custom fields, example data, the sign-up providers and the second factor are **ADR 0084 parts 1 and 2** and `docs/runbooks/add-a-business-type.md` (job CK); nothing here repeats them, and where this ADR and ADR 0084 disagree, ADR 0084 wins.

## Decision

1. **The web keeps no copy of the packs.** It asks the API: the sign-up cards (`GET /v1/packs`), the ways to sign in (`GET /v1/auth/providers`), the second-factor status (`GET /v1/account/security`), and for one workspace its pack and words (`GET /v1/tenants/{id}/pack`) and its modules (`GET .../modules`, the answer the database enforces; it wins over the pack's default list). All of it goes through one adapter (`lib/api/pack.ts`) that validates every field. A server without those routes (404, 501, 503 `packs_unavailable`) gives *no pack*, and the product is then exactly what it was before business types. The tests read the real files of `packages/packs` and put them in the API's shapes (`e2e/visual/packs-wire.mjs`, shared with the visual stand-in).
2. **Words come from the pack, over the dictionary.** The menu and the phone's tabs read `nav.*` and `pack.core_tab`; a screen that takes a translator reads `agent.*`/`word.*` through `withPackT`. A pack value replaces a dictionary value only for a key the page already has, so a pack changes words and never adds screen text.
3. **The phone's bar has one rule, for every business type:** Today, Leads, the business type's **core tab**, Visits, Office. The core tab (`core_tab`, a module code) takes the Quotes slot, and is called by the pack's own word (Quotes, Orders & deliveries, Fees). With no business type the bar is the one the product always had. *Not* the blueprint's `Today, Inbox, core, Contacts, More`: the owner's rule is that textiles looks exactly as today, and the pack files carry no per-type bar (only `core_tab` and `nav.tab.visits`). Changing the bar is one line in `components/v2/app/nav.ts` (`barItems`).
4. **A module that is off is hidden, refused with a 403 page, and the API's `module_off` is the same page.** The menu offers only items whose module is on. A typed address answers the 403 page (`forbidden()`, from the workspace's `template.tsx`, which Next.js remakes on every move between pages; a small gate in the layout covers a move by the router, which does not remake it). An API refusal `403 module_off` goes to the same page (`escalate` in `lib/api/failure.ts`) or, inside a form, to one sentence of ours. The 403 page's HTTP status is 200 (the workspace streams its loading shell first, as for every `notFound()`); the API and the database remain the gate.
5. **Textiles is not touched.** A workspace that existed before business types, or was made without choosing one (`source` `legacy` or `default`), keeps the first-run wizard it always had, no "example data" offer, and a Settings with no Fields tab until it has a field of its own; the pack's words equal the dictionary's in all four languages (a test proves it), and `today-default-pack` must read exactly like `today`. A workspace made at sign-up with a chosen business type (`signup`, or `operator`) gets the checklist.
6. **The first-run checklist is derived from the modules a workspace has**, never from a business type's name or a list in the pack (the pack files carry no checklist): name and city; products (a catalogue) or people (programmes); the delivery rule, else the quote rules, else the fee plan; the second step; WhatsApp (an inbox); the first quote, else the first reminder campaign. Each step is a real read; a step whose screen does not exist shows "Not available yet" and is never counted.
7. **Two-step sign-up**: the kind of business (cards from the API, "Not validated by a real owner yet" under a type no real owner has reviewed), then Google, Facebook (only the ones the server offers) or e-mail, and "By continuing you agree to the Terms and the Privacy Policy". The setup sends the terms versions for a provider account (it has no sign-up form to carry them); the business is called "My business" until checklist step 1.
8. **The second factor is asked in context, not at sign-up.** A checklist step before WhatsApp and the first approval, a note on the WhatsApp page while the session is at the first level, and the existing "needs your second step" sentence when the database refuses (`mfa_required`).

## Consequences and what is not decided here

* `next.config.ts` turns on `experimental.authInterrupts` (Next.js 16) for `forbidden()`. If a later Next.js drops it, the one call in `template.tsx` is replaced (the check is `moduleForPath` + `moduleOn`, plain functions with tests).
* Saree wording remains on about 60 screens and in the catalogue editor; per-type wording of those belongs to the jobs that build the screens (CN/CO construction, CR/CS education). Custom fields are drawn under the editor.
* Fees and Campaigns are placeholder pages ("Not available yet") until their jobs; their modules exist in the closed list.
* Accent colours and owl props are art tokens the web knows by name (`rose_silk`, `terracotta`, `sky_blue`; `fabric_roll`, `hard_hat_paver_block`, `graduation_cap`); a token it does not know draws the default look and a neutral prop (the runbook says a new one needs the web job).
* The route-JS ceilings were raised on 2026-10-14 by the owner (Today 204,000 B, the landing page 156,000 B) to leave room for waves 2 and 3; a trim job follows.
