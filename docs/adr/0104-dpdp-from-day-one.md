# ADR 0104: DPDP from day one (job DQ, part 4)

Status: built on branch `api/dq-dpdp` (night run 27 Oct 2026). Engineering decisions, not legal advice; the owner confirms the current DPDP Rules before the first real-data customer (`docs/runbooks/real-data-gate.md`, `docs/runbooks/breach.md`).

## Decision
1. **Consent is a ledger, not a flag.** `privacy.consent_events` is append-only: who, which purpose, given or withdrawn, the notice version, how (sign-up, Google, settings, prompt) and when. Two purposes: `service` (needed for the account; written when the notice is accepted; it cannot be withdrawn without ending the account, and the table refuses a withdrawal) and `marketing` (separate, off until the person turns it on). Giving and withdrawing are the SAME one call (`PUT /v1/me/consents/marketing`), so withdrawing is exactly as easy as giving. The notice version is the server's, never the browser's. A change is also written to the audit trail of each of the person's workspaces (the purpose and the action, never a value).
2. **The existing rights stay; three change.** Download (job BN) now also holds CSV beside JSON (a spreadsheet opens it; a text that would run as a formula gets an apostrophe) and the person's consent ledger; the cancel window of an account or workspace deletion is **7 days** (was 14); a person can correct their display name and see every change in the trail. Not new: access, erasure, retention settings, a workspace grievance.
3. **Two public doors, with the caps in the database.** `POST /v1/privacy/grievance` (anyone) and `POST /v1/public/waitlist` (the public site) have no sign-in, so their database functions are open to the public key. That key is public, so the API's per-address and per-contact limits are NOT the only fence: the functions hold their own caps (five grievances per contact per day, three hundred of each per hour), validate every field, write only their own table and return nothing that was stored. Captcha-ready: an interface with `none` (today) and `turnstile` (verifies with Cloudflare's siteverify, fails closed); no Cloudflare account is made here.
4. **The e-mails belong to the worker.** The API stores; the worker sends (privacy inbox, the person's acknowledgement, hello inbox) through the one e-mail sender, marks each send, and tries again after a failure. No request ever waits on a mail provider and the provider key stays in the worker (and API) environments only. The inbox addresses are `PRIVACY_EMAIL` and `HELLO_EMAIL`, never literals in code.
5. **A lapsed workspace is warned, then erased, behind its own switch.** Read-only for 90 days (job DI), an e-mail to every Owner 48 hours before, then the existing tenant erasure (`app.erase_tenant`) by the scheduler. The database re-checks everything at erase time (switch on, billing enforced, not exempt, 90 days over, a warning for THIS erasure moment at least 48 hours old) and refuses otherwise. A workspace with no Owner address is never marked warned, so never erased. **The switch (`lapse_erasure`) is OFF** and needs billing enforcement on as well: turning billing on must never erase anything in the first minute, and the operator reads the list first (`docs/runbooks/dpdp-operations.md`). Exempt workspaces (Customer Zero, the demos) are never touched.
6. **Retention of logs.** Audit rows older than 395 days (a year, plus a month's grace) are purged by the worker, once an hour, through the same door the per-workspace retention uses. Resolved public grievances go a year after they close; waitlist rows 12 months after they joined.
7. **Subprocessors are data** (`GET /v1/privacy/subprocessors`, a JSON file): Google Cloud, Supabase, Resend, Zoho, Anthropic, Meta/WhatsApp and Sarvam, each with its purpose, the country and the data. A first draft for the owner to check against each provider's terms.

## Consequences
- The privacy page and the sign-up form can show a person's consents and read the notice facts from the API instead of hard-coding them (C2).
- Direct calls to the two public functions with the public key are possible; the damage is bounded by the caps and by validation, and no e-mail is sent for them (the worker only mails what is stored).
- Operator work remains: switching `lapse_erasure` on, answering grievances (the purge clock starts when the operator marks one resolved), and confirming the Rules and the Board's notification channel.
