# ADR 0092: Plans as data, usage meters, enforcement hooks, and a billing provider interface with a fake provider

Status: **built and tested locally (job DI, night run C1, 2026-10-25); nothing deployed; no account opened; no network call to any billing provider exists.** Prices are NOT decided (every price is `null`). Enforcement is built but its platform switch is OFF. Builds on ADR 0063/0064 (AI allowances and fair use, unchanged), ADR 0066/0076 (the outbox and WhatsApp), ADR 0084 part 6 (the billing sketch, which this partly replaces), ADR 0017 (a paid dependency needs the owner's written approval).

## Context
The owner decided on 23 Oct 2026 (draft, to be tuned from usage tests): main plans **Starter / Growth / Business**, calls-only plans **Pay-as-you-go / Calls 300 / Calls 1,000**, a **30-day trial with a payment method at the start**. Only two things are metered and shown to owners: **AI call minutes** and **business-initiated WhatsApp messages**. AI itself is "included, fair use" with the existing internal allowances (ADR 0063/0064), never shown in rupees or tokens. Draft allowances: Trial 30 min / 100 messages; Starter 0 / 300; Growth 100 / 1,000; Business 300 / 3,000; Calls 300 = 300 min; Calls 1,000 = 1,000 min; users 1/1/3/10, workspaces 1/1/1/3 for Trial/Starter/Growth/Business.

## Decisions
1. **Plans are data**, like packs: `packages/plans/plans.json` shaped by `plans.schema.json`; `make plans-check` validates it (rules: one trial plan, unique ids, the main plans grow, pay-as-you-go only for a calls-only plan), refuses **a plan id as a string literal on a line about a plan** in the API or the web (three old files are listed with a reason in `scripts/plan_id_allowlist.txt`), and fails when the version is not in a migration. `make plans-migration` writes the migration from the file (`billing.plans`, `billing.catalogue_versions`, `billing.plan_versions`); **a synced version is never edited** (a database trigger and the script both refuse); a change is a new `version`. `tenants.plan`, `plan_ai_allowances` and `plan_voice_allowances` now **reference the catalogue** instead of holding a CHECK list of names (the three calls-only plans carry a placeholder internal AI allowance equal to Starter's, never shown, and no voice notes).
2. **Meters in the database**, per workspace and per period: `billing_usage_events` (units: seconds for calls, 1 per message; one row per call or message, so a retry counts once), `billing_topups` (operator only for now), `billing_notices`. The **period is the workspace's month window of the AI fair-use meter** (`app.ai_month_window`, India dates, anchored on the trial start or the paid-period start), so both meters turn over on the same day. A message is counted by a trigger **when the outbox marks it sent**, if it is not a reply inside the customer's 24-hour window (`reply_to_inbound`); call seconds are handed in by the registered **channel account** (the worker that also receives webhooks), never by an Owner. RLS: Owner and Admin read; nobody writes directly; ledgers cannot be changed, deleted or truncated; every table is audited.
3. **What an owner reads** (`GET /billing`, `/billing/usage`): counts, allowance, remaining, a whole percent, a state (`ok` under 80, `notice` from 80, `stopped` at 100 or when the plan includes none) and the notices. **Never AI rupees, tokens or the ledger** (a test scans the generated contract and a real answer). The contract is `packages/contracts/billing.*` with mock payloads and `docs/contracts/plans-billing.md`.
4. **Notices:** one row per kind, level (80, 100) and period, made once when the line is crossed.
5. **Enforcement hooks**, behind the platform switch `billing_enforced` (**OFF by default**; `app.operator_set_billing_enforced(true)`): at 100 percent (or when none is included) that kind stops for the workspace until a top-up or the next period. **Business-initiated messages:** the outbox's claim (`app.outbox_claim_due_core`) does not hand them out, so they stay **queued, untouched, nothing lost**; a reply inside the 24-hour window is not held. **Call minutes:** the call start asks `public.billing_check_usage` (Python: `UsageGate.ensure_allowed`, which raises `plan_limit_reached`). **Quotes, orders, follow-ups and customers never consult the meter** (a pgTAP test lists the only two functions that do). AI fair use is unchanged.
   *Why OFF by default:* with the switch on, every existing workspace (all on the 100-message trial allowance, on a catalogue that is explicitly a draft) would start holding business messages after 100 a month, on staging and in the demos. The meters and notices run regardless, so the numbers can be watched before the switch is turned on. **This is the owner's decision at go-live.**
6. **The provider interface** (`app/billing/provider.py`): `create_subscription` (registers the subscription with a trial; charges nothing; refuses a payment method that cannot be authorised), `cancel_subscription`, `events_until` (the fake's clock) and `verify_webhook` (a real provider's signed events). The provider tells us **events** (`charge_succeeded`, `charge_failed`, `halted`, `cancelled`), each with an id; the database applies each id once. Only `FakeBillingProvider` exists (`BILLING_PROVIDER=fake`; anything else stops the process at start). Its payment-method references choose the outcome: `pm_fake_ok`, `pm_fake_declined`, `pm_fake_declines_on_renewal`, `pm_fake_declines_after_first`.
7. **The subscription state machine** (`billing_subscriptions`): `trialing` -> (first charge ok) `active` | (fails) `past_due` -> (retry ok) `active` | (provider gives up) `cancelled`. While trialing, the **trial plan's allowances apply** whatever plan was chosen; the chosen plan starts with the first successful charge (`tenants.plan` and `billing_anchor_at` follow, and with them the AI window and allowance). **Cancel:** during the trial, or after a failed payment, it ends at once and nothing is ever charged; on an active plan it ends at the end of the paid period. A late event never revives a cancelled subscription. **Order of the two systems:** start = read, provider, record (the provider's subscription is cancelled if the record fails); cancel = provider first, then record. Provider events are applied only by the **channel account** (an Owner cannot give itself a plan).
8. **A charge is never invented:** with no price set, the fake provider produces no charge event at all.

## What the real Razorpay adapter needs (checked on Razorpay's documentation, 2026-10-25)
Facts read from razorpay.com/docs: a **plan** sets price and schedule and a **subscription** per customer is charged automatically; trial periods and upfront charges are plan options; UPI AutoPay works with the major UPI apps, e-mandate and cards are also supported; after a failed charge a card or UPI subscription is retried once a day on T+1, T+2 and T+3 (`subscription.pending`), then moves to `halted` (`subscription.halted`) and the customer must change the payment method; the subscription webhooks are `authenticated`, `activated`, `charged`, `pending`, `halted`, `cancelled`, `completed`, `paused`, `resumed`, `updated`; the signature is checked over the **raw** body; Subscriptions are available in India. **Not confirmed from those pages (verify before building):** how a trial start is set (`start_at`), the authorisation transaction and its amount, per-debit limits for UPI AutoPay without an extra authentication step, the pre-debit notification rules, the signature header name and algorithm. Our grace period (7 days) and the fake's retry timing are OUR rules and are not Razorpay's.

**The owner must set up at Razorpay (nothing of this has been done):**
1. A Razorpay business account with KYC complete (live mode needs it), the **Subscriptions** product and **UPI AutoPay / recurring** enabled for the account (ask Razorpay support what they require), the checkout features Razorpay names as a prerequisite.
2. The plans in Razorpay (one per plan we sell and per price, created by the adapter or by hand), once the prices are decided.
3. **Test-mode** keys first (Key ID and Key Secret), then live keys. The Key Secret and the webhook secret go to Google Secret Manager (proposed names `sme-razorpay-key-secret`, `sme-razorpay-webhook-secret`, and the Key ID in `sme-razorpay-key-id`); never in Git, logs or reports; the browser gets only the Key ID.
4. A webhook in the Razorpay dashboard pointing at `https://<api host>/v1/webhooks/billing/razorpay` with the ten subscription events above, and its secret.
5. Business details for invoices (the company's legal name, GSTIN, address) and a decision on who issues GST invoices (Razorpay's subscription invoices or ours). The lawyer/CA decision on GST and on the payment terms of the trial belongs here.
6. The fee Razorpay charges per transaction and for UPI AutoPay is part of the price decision; it is not a fixed monthly cost to us, but the owner should read the current pricing page before approving (ADR 0017).
Then, in code: a `RazorpayProvider` implementing the four methods above (httpx, a signed-body check, the ten events mapped to our four), a new migration widening `billing_subscriptions.provider` to `'razorpay'` and `public.billing_start_trial`, the webhook route using the channel account, and a contract test against Razorpay's test mode.

## Consequences
* New: schema `billing` (no client privileges), five `public.billing_*` tables, the functions listed in the migration (nine callable by a signed-in account, each proving role or the channel account first), SQLSTATEs SM821 to SM823, the platform flag `billing_enforced`, the setting `BILLING_PROVIDER`, the routes above.
* `app.outbox_claim_due_core` was redefined (a full copy plus one condition). Existing guards (`06`, `75`, `69`, `93`, flags tests) were updated for the new tables, the foreign key on `tenants.plan` (a bad plan is now `23503`, not `23514`) and the three extra AI allowance rows.
* **Not built:** a purchase flow for top-ups, a plan-change (upgrade/downgrade) flow, invoices and GST numbering (ADR 0084 part 6c), the webhook route, a hard stop when a trial ends without payment (undecided), the enforcement of the users/workspaces limits by plan (the data holds them; `tenants.workspace_limit` still decides), the web screens (C2 builds them against the contract).
* Voice notes (ADR 0088) keep their own fair-use meter in seconds and are **not** counted as call minutes (a different thing; an owner decision if it should change).
