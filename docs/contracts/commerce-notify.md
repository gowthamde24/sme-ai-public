# Contract: commerce, notifications out, calendars in, lead-form templates (C1 -> C2), 25 Nov 2026

Status: built on branch `sa/commerce-notify`, nothing deployed. Every piece is behind its own switch and OFF by default (production stays off until the owner's gate):
`COMMERCE` (B), `NOTIFY_OUT` (C), `CALENDAR_IN` (D), `LEAD_FORM_TEMPLATES` (A). While a switch is off every route of that piece answers 404 (the web hides the cards). Credentials never go to the
database: they live in the workspace-secrets store (`WORKSPACE_SECRETS`, Secret Manager in production) as one JSON value per workspace and connector (`app/connector_vault.py`). No test reaches a vendor:
every vendor call goes through `app/outbound_http.GuardedHttp` (SSRF guard, no redirects, size and time limits) and the tests give it a fake transport.

Words used on the cards (all pieces): state is `connected`, `not_connected` or `needs_setup` (= "Needs setup by us": the Stack19 side has not finished a step only the Stack19 owner can do, for example registering an OAuth app).
Roles: Owner and Admin connect, change and disconnect; every member reads the state (never a secret, a token or an address: only a label and the last four characters).

Vendor facts below are taken from the vendors' public documentation (URLs cited per section). Where the documentation was unclear the text says **verify with a real account**; nothing is invented.

## A. Lead-form templates (switch `LEAD_FORM_TEMPLATES`)
Seven cards for the Lead sources page, each with the steps (plain words), a sample body in the vendor's real shape, how the fields are mapped, how consent is sent and what is still unverified. Data: `app/lead_sources/data/form_templates.json`; readers: `app/lead_sources/form_templates.py`. With the switch off nothing here is read (a vendor body is passed on untouched, TradeIndia mails get no reader of their own) and the two routes answer 404.

### Routes (Owner, Admin; 404 while `LEAD_FORM_TEMPLATES` is off)
* `GET /v1/tenants/{t}/lead-sources/form-templates` -> `{templates: [{id, name, group: "forms"|"mail", channel: "json"|"multipart"|"mail"}], note}`. Ids: `google_ads`, `typeform`, `tally`, `jotform`, `linkedin_lead_gen`, `webflow`, `tradeindia`.
* `GET /v1/tenants/{t}/lead-sources/form-templates/{id}` -> `{id, name, group, channel, docs: [url], steps: [text], sample: {...}, mapping: {their field: our field}, consent: text, verify: [text]}`. The lead address is written into `steps` (the real URL when the API knows its public base, else `.../YOUR-ADDRESS`); the consent time in `sample` is fresh.
* The address a tool posts to is the workspace's ordinary lead address (a `webhook` source, `POST /v1/lead-intake/{token}`), except **Jotform**, which posts a multipart form and so uses `POST /v1/lead-intake/{token}/jotform` (the same secret address plus `/jotform`; no sign-in; 422 for anything that is not a multipart form with a `rawRequest` part). Rate rule: the existing `lead_intake` rule.

### What each reader does
A reader acts only when the body is unmistakably that vendor's envelope. It builds the flat body the intake already understands (`name, phone, email, message, city, external_id, consent, consent_text, consent_at, consent_form`); everything else goes to the lead's message as `question: answer` (at most 12).
* **Consent.** The form has ONE consent question: a yes/no or checkbox question whose name contains the word "consent" (Typeform: the question's reference or title; Tally: the label; Webflow: the field name; Jotform: the unique name; Google Ads: the column). The question's own text is the wording stored; the time is the vendor's submit time (Typeform `submitted_at`, Tally `createdAt`, Webflow `submittedAt`, Google `lead_submit_time`) or, where the vendor documents none (Jotform), the time we received it. The intake's existing rule decides whether a tick is believed (`LeadSourcesService.consent_evidence`: wording 10 to 800 characters, time not in the future and not older than 30 days). A body without the consent question, with an unticked one or with an old time is still a lead, with **no consent stored: ask first**.
* **Duplicates.** The vendor's response id becomes `external_id` (Google `lead_id`, Typeform `token`, Tally `responseId`, Webflow `payload.id`, Jotform `submissionID`), so a delivery repeated by the vendor is one lead.
* **Google Ads `google_key`.** The advertiser types this key in Google Ads and Google sends it with every lead; the documentation says the receiver should compare it. We do NOT: the lead address is a long secret and is itself the credential, so the card tells the owner to type any word. The key is dropped and never stored or logged. `is_test: true` leads are kept, with the message "Test lead from Google Ads".
* **LinkedIn Lead Gen** has no webhook of its own: the template is the Zapier (or Make) recipe plus the flat body its "Webhooks by Zapier" / HTTP step sends; a body with `first_name` and `last_name` but no `name` gets them joined.
* **TradeIndia** is an e-mail: read by `Label: value` lines (buyer name, mobile, e-mail, city, product requirement), through the e-mail lead address; its own labels are tried before the portals' reader. No consent comes with a portal mail (ask first).

## B. Shopify and WooCommerce (switch `COMMERCE`)

Code: `services/ai-api/app/commerce/`; migration `20261231992340_commerce_stores.sql` (tables `commerce_connections`, `commerce_orders`, `commerce_checkouts`; pgTAP `293_commerce_stores.test.sql`, written, not run locally).
Needs `WORKSPACE_SECRETS` (the store's keys), `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` (https: the stores post to it) and the scheduler account. Without the secret store a connect answers 503 `commerce_unavailable`.

### What it does
1. **Connect with the store's own keys.** Shopify: shop address (`my-shop.myshopify.com`), the app's **client secret** (it also signs the store's webhooks) and EITHER an Admin API access token (an app made before Shopify stopped
   allowing new admin-created custom apps) OR the app's client id (an app made in the Dev Dashboard: we exchange client id + secret for a 24 hour token with the client credentials grant, and renew it). WooCommerce: the site's https address,
   a consumer key and secret (read/write). We check the keys (Shopify `{ shop { name } }`, Woo `GET /products?per_page=1`), make a webhook address for the store (`<PUBLIC_BASE_URL>/v1/webhooks/commerce/<shopify|woocommerce>/<long secret>`; only
   its sha256 is in the database), keep the keys in the vault, and register the webhooks through the store's API (Shopify GraphQL `webhookSubscriptionCreate`; Woo `POST /wp-json/wc/v3/webhooks` with a secret we make). If the store refuses the registration
   the card says `problem: webhooks_not_registered` and the answer carries `manual_setup` (the address, and for Woo the secret, shown only then) so the owner can add them by hand. Test repeats the checks and the registration (an existing webhook is left alone).
2. **Events.** Shopify topics `orders/create`, `orders/updated`, `orders/cancelled`, `orders/fulfilled`, `checkouts/create`, `checkouts/update`; WooCommerce `order.created`, `order.updated`. The body's signature is checked on the RAW body before anything is read
   (Shopify: `X-Shopify-Hmac-SHA256`, base64 HMAC-SHA256 with the app's client secret; Woo: `X-WC-Webhook-Signature`, base64 HMAC-SHA256 with the webhook's secret): wrong or missing = 401, nothing stored. Unknown address or kind = 404. 200 for anything understood,
   ignored or repeated; 503 only when our side failed (the store retries; Shopify deletes an API-made subscription after 8 failures in a row, so a failure is kept rare). Body at most 512 KB. A repeat delivery changes nothing (the order is keyed by the store's id; the lead by `external_id`).
3. **Orders become Stack19 jobs the safe way.** An order is stored (number, status, how it is paid, total in paise, item names, tracking, the customer's number only so the phone assistant can find the caller's own orders; never an address). Then, by the owner's choice
   `confirm_orders` (`cod` default, `all`, `off`), a lead is posted through the ordinary lead intake on a webhook source made for the store (`LeadSourcesService.receive_form`): name, number, city, and a message such as `Online order #1001 (cash on delivery, Rs 1,299): Silk saree x2.
   Confirm the order and the delivery address with the customer.` From there the DATABASE decides, exactly as for every other lead: consent, do-not-call list, calling hours, the daily call limit, the wallet, duplicates, and the assistant's first touch (the owner's own rule for new leads: call or
   WhatsApp). **Consent:** shown only when the owner ticked `checkout_consent` ("my checkout asks customers to agree that I may call or message them about their order"; who ticked and when are kept); then the lead carries the store's say-so with the order's time. Otherwise the lead is
   **ask first** and nothing is called. A cancelled or returned order never makes a lead. A dedicated COD script or a Meta-approved WhatsApp order template is NOT built here (the first touch is the assistant's ordinary one with the order in the message): see the owner steps.
4. **Abandoned cart (Shopify only; WooCommerce has no abandoned-cart event of its own).** With `abandoned` on, a checkout that has a phone number waits `abandoned_after_min` (15 to 1440, default 60). A later `orders/create` carrying the checkout's token, or a checkout with `completed_at`, cancels the wait.
   The worker (`COMMERCE_PASS`, once per tick) turns a due checkout into a lead ("Left a cart at the online store (...). Offer to help finish the order.") the same safe way, then wipes the number and the name. Checkouts are kept at most 7 days, orders 180 days.
5. **Order-status answers on the phone.** When a caller's number matches orders of a connected store (and `share_status` is on, the default), the call's fact pack gets up to three lines such as `This caller's own order #1001 (cash on delivery, Rs 1,299, Silk saree x2): sent. Tracking: ...`.
   Only the caller's own number is looked up; no address, name or e-mail is in a line; no date is promised; the total is added to the pack's allowed amounts so the money guard lets it be said. Inbound calls only (an outbound call never gets it). Caller-ID can be faked: the lines carry nothing an order's own confirmation e-mail would not.
6. **Catalogue import (preview, then save).** `POST .../catalogue/preview {list_id}` reads the store's active products (Shopify GraphQL `products`, at most 1,000 rows; Woo `products?per_page=100`) and maps them to the chosen list of kind Products: the price into its default price column in whole paise (never guessed: a missing
   or non-numeric price is left empty with a note), availability into one of the list's own choice words (out of stock, few left when 1 to 3 are in stock, else in stock), the description into its description column. Notes (closed): `check_currency` (amounts are taken as rupees: look before saving), `price_missing`, `name_missing`, `availability_word_missing`, `no_price_column`, `variable_products_one_row`, `too_many`, `partial`.
   Nothing is saved. `POST .../catalogue/save {list_id, rows}` sends the rows the owner kept to the lists' own import (`rows_import`): all or nothing, a known name updates that item, only the Owner may set a price (an Admin saving prices gets 403). Needs `WHAT_YOU_SELL_LISTS`.

### Routes (prefix `/v1/tenants/{t}/commerce`; Owner or Admin write, every member reads; no DELETE verbs)
* `GET /connections` -> `{items: [{id, kind: shopify|woocommerce, store_label, state: connected|problem, problem: null|bad_credentials|no_permission|unreachable|webhooks_not_registered|signature_wrong, secret_last4, webhooks_registered, last_event_at, settings, abandoned_supported, counts: {orders_30d, cod_30d, carts_waiting}, created_at}], settings_words}`. Never a key, a token or the address.

* `POST /connections/woocommerce {id, site, key, secret}` -> 201 the card (+ `manual_setup {address, secret, note}` when needed).

* `POST /v1/webhooks/commerce/{kind}/{token}` (public; the store's signature).
* Closed refusals: 422 `store_address_invalid`, `credentials_invalid`, `store_refused` (`reason`: bad_credentials | no_permission | unreachable | not_found | rate_limited), `invalid_value`; 409 `list_not_products`; 503 `commerce_unavailable`, `public_address_missing`; 404 while the switch is off.

### What a business must give the store (for the card's steps)

## C. Notifications out: Slack, Telegram, Microsoft Teams (switch `NOTIFY_OUT`)
Migration `20261231992345_notify_out.sql`; code `services/ai-api/app/notify_out/`; tests `tests/test_notify_out.py`, pgTAP `292_notify_out.test.sql`. Base `/v1/tenants/{t}/notify/channels`. Off: every route 404. No secret store (`WORKSPACE_SECRETS` off): the routes answer 503 `notify_out_unavailable` and the worker sends nothing.

**What it is.** One channel per workspace and kind (`slack`, `telegram`, `teams`). The Owner pastes the vendor's address or token on the card; it goes to the vault (`sme-ws-<workspace>-conn-<kind>`, JSON, Secret Manager in production) and is never returned, logged or stored in the database (the database keeps the Owner's label, the last four characters, the chosen events and fields, the language and the channel's health). Every message goes out through `app/outbound_http.GuardedHttp` (https only, name resolved once and every answer public, no redirect, 5 s connect and 10 s read, pinned to the vendor's own host names: `hooks.slack.com`, `api.telegram.org`, `*.logic.azure.com` or `*.powerplatform.com`).

### Routes (Owner or Admin write; every member reads)
* `GET ""` (any member) -> `{items: [3 channels], kinds, events, fields, limits}`.
  * `items[]` (always slack, telegram, teams, in that order): `{kind, state: "connected"|"not_connected", label, last4, events: ["new_lead"|"missed_call"|"call_outcome"|"low_balance"], fields: ["name"|"phone_masked"|"outcome"|"reason"], language: "en"|"te"|"hi"|"kn", test_passed, health: {status: "ok"|"problem"|"never_sent", reason: closed word|null, last_sent_at}, counts_24h: {sent, failed, rate_limited}}`. `rate_limited` counts messages dropped by the rate limit and the collapse.
  * `kinds[]`: `{id, name, fields: [{key, label, hint}]}`: the labelled plain fields of each card (Slack: `url`; Telegram: `token`, `chat_id`; Teams: `url`) with one hint line each. Draw the card from this; no sentence form.
  * `events[]`: `{id, words}`; `fields[]`: `{id, words, default}` (defaults: name, phone_masked, outcome); `limits`: `{per_minute: 20, per_hour: 300, lead_messages_per_minute: 5}`.
* `PUT /{kind}` `{label? (<=60), url? | token? + chat_id?, events? (subset of the four), fields? (subset of the four), language? (en|te|hi|kn)}` -> `{channel}` (the `items[]` shape). Connect or change. Secret fields are optional on a change (the stored one stays); a new secret makes the channel untested again; a Telegram `chat_id` can change alone. The first connect needs the secret fields. `events` left out = as it was (none on a new channel).
* `POST /{kind}/test` -> `{ok, reason, channel}`: sends ONE fixed line now ("Test message from Stack19. This channel is connected.", no personal data) and records the result. A pass records the time and, the first time and only when the Owner chose no events, switches the four events on. At most one test per channel each 10 seconds (429 `asked_too_soon`). 404 when the channel is not connected.
* `POST /{kind}/disconnect` -> `{disconnected: true}`: removes the channel, its log and its secret (the API has no DELETE verbs). 404 when not connected.
* Unknown `kind`: 404. Unknown event, field or language, or an extra key: 422.

### Closed refusals
`422 channel_invalid` with `error.reason` (plain `message` too): `secret_required`; `slack_url` (not `https://hooks.slack.com/services/T.../B.../...`); `telegram_token` (not `<digits>:<30-50 letters, digits, - _>`); `telegram_chat` (not a number or `@name`); `teams_url` (https, host must end `.logic.azure.com` or `.powerplatform.com`); `teams_legacy` (an Office 365 connector address: `*.webhook.office.com`, `outlook.office.com`); `choices` (the database refused an event or field). `503 notify_out_unavailable`; `403` for Sales and Viewers on every write.
`health.reason` (and the failed message's reason) is one of: `bad_address` (404, 410, 401 and, for Slack and Teams, 403: the address or token no longer works; reconnect), `rejected` (another 4xx), `rate_limited` (429), `timeout`, `refused` (cannot connect, or the name does not resolve), `tls`, `server` (5xx), `blocked` (the address is not the vendor's, or points at a private address), `no_secret` (the stored secret is gone: reconnect), `redirect` (never followed). The vendor's own words are never kept or shown.

### What a message says
Built in code from closed phrases (English; Telugu, Hindi and Kannada are **drafts for native review**, te for the owner's review per the repo rule) and ONLY the fields the Owner allowed. Default: name + phone as its first three digits and X's (`+91 987XXXXXXX`) + the outcome word. `reason` only when ticked, with e-mail addresses and runs of five digits or more taken out and cut to 140 characters. A missed call from someone who is not a contact shows `XXXXXXX` and the last four digits at most. Never: a full number, an e-mail address, an address, a transcript, an amount, the lead's free text. The database function `app.notify_event_data` returns only those closed fields, read when the message is sent (an erased person leaves nothing), and the API re-checks them (`messages.safe_data`) before putting them in a sentence. Examples: `New lead: Ravi Kumar, +91 987XXXXXXX` · `Missed call: Unknown caller, XXXXXXX4321` · `Call result: Ravi Kumar, +91 987XXXXXXX - Interested` · `Your call balance is low. Top up so calls do not stop.` · `and 12 more new leads`. Slack text is escaped (`& < >`) so a name cannot mention the channel; Teams text has its Markdown marks removed; Telegram is sent as plain text.

### Events, limits, retries
* `new_lead` (a lead is created; test leads are skipped), `missed_call` (a call to the business number is missed), `call_outcome` (an outcome of kind call is recorded; WhatsApp follow-up outcomes are not), `low_balance` (the same test as Today's "balance is low" item, `pricing.is_low`, scanned by the worker every 10 minutes: ONE message per low episode, and again only after a top-up and a new low). Queued by triggers only for a channel that asked; a failure to queue never fails the lead, call or outcome.
* Rate limit per channel, counted in the database at claim time: at most 20 messages a minute and 300 an hour; a message over that is dropped as `rate_limited` (counted on the card, never queued forever). More than 5 new-lead messages in a minute: the rest are collapsed into ONE message ("and N more new leads") sent on the next tick. The worker also paces sends to one channel (Slack and Telegram 1 s apart, Teams 0.3 s).
* Retries: a message is tried, then again after 1 minute, 5 minutes and 30 minutes; then it has failed and the card shows a Problem with the closed reason. `bad_address`, `rejected`, `blocked`, `no_secret` and `redirect` are never retried. The log is kept 30 days.
* Worker: `NotifyRunner` in `scheduler/worker.py`, only when `NOTIFY_OUT=on` and the secret store exists.

## D. Calendar and booking: Calendly, Cal.com, Outlook / Microsoft 365 (switch `CALENDAR_IN`)
Code: `services/ai-api/app/calendar_in/`, migration `20261231992360_calendar_in.sql`, pgTAP `296_calendar_in.test.sql` (written, not run locally), `tests/test_calendar_in.py`. Off unless `CALENDAR_IN=on` (every route, the booking door and the worker answer 404 / do nothing). Needs `WORKSPACE_SECRETS` (the keys), `PUBLIC_BASE_URL` (https), `PUBLIC_WEB_BASE_URL` and the scheduler account. Google Calendar is not part of this (it is `docs/contracts/google-calendar.md`).

### What it does
- **Calendly and Cal.com bookings come in.** A booking made on the owner's own Calendly or Cal.com page reaches a long secret address of ours; the vendor's signature on the raw body is checked; the invitee becomes a **lead** through the normal lead intake (so consent, Do-Not-Call, hours and caps are decided by the database exactly as for every other source) and a **visit** through the visits engine (`app.visit_claim_slot`: opening hours, closed days, slot grid, capacity, staff, the buffer and every busy block, Google's and Outlook's included). Nothing is forced in: a booking that does not fit a free slot is kept as `needs_person` with a closed reason (`no_lead`, `closed`, `slot`, `full`, `past`) and the lead is still created; the visits calendar shows nothing for it, the cards show a count and the web lists them (`GET .../bookings/needs-person`). **There is no "Needs you" feed entry for it yet** (the Today and nav-count functions are not touched by this slice): say so on the card.
- The same vendor booking id twice is one visit; a cancel cancels the visit that came from it once; a "created" that arrives after its own cancel is ignored; a reschedule is the old booking cancelled and the new time placed (Calendly sends `invitee.canceled` and a new `invitee.created` whose `old_invitee` names the old one; Cal.com sends `BOOKING_RESCHEDULED` with the new `uid` and `rescheduleUid`).
- **Consent stays strict.** A booking is the invitee asking to be met, not permission to be called or messaged. The lead is created without consent (ask first) unless the owner names ONE question of their booking form as the consent question (`consent_question`, 10 to 200 characters, their own words, set in the card's settings): a non-empty answer that is not a no counts, and the question's wording plus the time of the booking are stored as the evidence (`consent`, `consent_text`, `consent_at`, `consent_form` on the lead intake, 30 days at most).
- **Our booking link out.** The owner saves their booking page address (https, on `calendly.com` for Calendly; `cal.com` or a `*.cal.com` name for Cal.com; nothing else is kept) and turns **Share my booking link** on (default off). Only then does `scheduler_calendar_booking_link(tenant)` answer, and the phone and WhatsApp assistants may say that exact address (`app/calendar_in/links.py: booking_link_fact(url, kind)` gives the fact `{"kind": "booking", "text": ...}`; `CalendarInService.booking_link(tenant_id)` reads it). **The hook is not wired into the fact pack here** (another builder is editing `fact_pack.py`): add two lines. In `app/telephony/inbound_store.py`, `InboundStore.fact_pack`, after `pack = out if isinstance(out, dict) else None`: `if pack is not None and calendar_in is not None: pack["booking_fact"] = calendar_in.booking_link(tenant_id)` (the runtime's `calendar_in`; it is None while the switch is off, and the call never waits on it: wrap it like the other reads). In `fact_pack.build`, after the hours facts: `if isinstance(raw.get("booking_fact"), dict): facts.append(raw["booking_fact"])`. The WhatsApp auto-reply takes the same fact from `CalendarInService.booking_link`.
- **Outlook / Microsoft 365** (Microsoft Graph, the user's default calendar), like Google Calendar but thinner: connect (OAuth, PKCE), **write events** (every appointment copied as "Appointment: Priya S." with only the purpose and the party size; never a number or an e-mail address; a retried create is made once through `transactionId`; a cancelled appointment deletes our event; only events whose id is in our table are ever changed), **block busy times** (calendarView of the next 60 days, our own events left out, `showAs` busy / oof / tentative count, stored as busy blocks with source `outlook` which the slot function already honours), **disconnect** (our copy of the key and every row is deleted; events already in Outlook stay). Not built: a push channel (Graph change notifications), so busy times refresh every 30 minutes; no "sync now" button; no calendar picker (default calendar only); no erased-workspace sweep for Outlook links.

### Vendor facts (read Nov 2026; URLs are the sources)
- **Calendly webhooks.** Subscription: `POST https://api.calendly.com/webhook_subscriptions` with `url`, `events`, `organization`, `scope` (`organization`, `user` or `group`), optional `signing_key` (a personal access token may set it); scope `webhooks:write`; the owner of the organization's plan decides which events are allowed (a `403` "Please upgrade to a paid Calendly account."): https://developer.calendly.com/api-docs/calendly-api/webhooks/create-webhook-subscription , https://developer.calendly.com/docs/api-guides/receive-data-from-scheduled-events-in-real-time-with-webhook-subscriptions . The Calendly FAQ says webhooks are on every plan and an organization-scope subscription needs an organization admin or owner: https://developer.calendly.com/docs/getting-started/frequently-asked-questions . Signature: header `Calendly-Webhook-Signature: t=<seconds>,v1=<hex>`, HMAC-SHA256 over `<t>.<raw body>` with the signing key; the vendor's example rejects a timestamp older than 3 minutes (we allow 5): https://developer.calendly.com/api-docs/overview/webhooks/webhook-signatures . The invitee's fields (`uri`, `email`, `name`, `first_name`, `last_name`, `status`, `questions_and_answers [{question, answer, position}]`, `text_reminder_number`, `rescheduled`, `old_invitee`, `new_invitee`, `cancellation`): https://developer.calendly.com/api-docs/calendly-api/scheduled-events/get-event-invitee . A webhook delivers `{event, created_at, payload}` with the invitee as `payload` and the meeting as `payload.scheduled_event {name, start_time, end_time}`: the vendor's own payload page was not reachable, so this shape is from a third-party description (https://cotera.co/docs/reference/webhooks/calendly): **verify with a real account**.
- **Calendly phone.** The invitee has a phone only if the event type asks for SMS reminders (`text_reminder_number`) or has a phone question. We read `text_reminder_number`, else the answer to a question whose text contains phone, mobile, whatsapp, contact number or cell. **Verify with a real account.**
- **Cal.com webhooks.** Header `X-Cal-Signature-256` = hex HMAC-SHA256 of the raw body with the webhook's secret (no timestamp), body `{triggerEvent, createdAt, payload}`; read from the sender's source, https://github.com/calcom/cal.com/blob/main/packages/features/webhooks/lib/sendPayload.ts , and https://cal.com/docs/developing/guides/automation/webhooks , https://cal.com/features/webhooks . Payload fields used: `uid`, `title`, `startTime`, `endTime`, `attendees [{name, email, timeZone}]`, `responses`, `smsReminderNumber`, `rescheduleUid`. The phone field's key in `responses` (`attendeePhoneNumber`) and whether `BOOKING_RESCHEDULED` carries the new `uid` (one public issue says it did not: https://github.com/calcom/cal.diy/issues/10631) are **verify with a real account**; if the new uid is missing the move cannot be told from a new booking and the old visit stays until its own cancel arrives. We register no Cal.com webhook through their API (the API route for it needs a plan and a key we do not ask for): the owner pastes the address and secret.
- **Microsoft identity platform v2 and Graph.** Authorization code with PKCE (S256) at `https://login.microsoftonline.com/common/oauth2/v2.0/authorize` and `/token` (`common` = work, school and personal accounts); `offline_access` must be asked for to get a refresh token; a refresh returns a new refresh token which replaces the old: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/v2-oauth2-auth-code-flow , https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity-platform/scopes-oidc . Scopes asked: `offline_access` and `https://graph.microsoft.com/Calendars.ReadWrite` (`User.Read` is not asked for: the profile is never read). Busy times: `GET /me/calendar/calendarView?startDateTime=&endDateTime=` with `Prefer: outlook.timezone="UTC"`, paged by `@odata.nextLink`, Calendars.ReadBasic is the least privilege and Calendars.ReadWrite covers it: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/user-list-calendarview . Events: `POST /me/events`, `PATCH` and `DELETE /me/events/{id}` and the event's `transactionId` (a client identifier that keeps a retried create from making a second event): https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/user-post-events , https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/graph/api/resources/event . **Verify with a real account:** that `transactionId` is honoured on a personal Outlook.com account as on a work account; the exact error words of an expired or revoked grant (we treat a 400 or 401 from the token endpoint as "connect again"); that calendarView `showAs` filtering matches what the owner sees for all-day and "working elsewhere" events.
